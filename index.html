01 / About me

I'm Ian Ding, a physics graduate from Heilongjiang University, China, who took a detour into C, C++ and Linux. As a kid, I was fascinated by astronomy and astrophysics; my physics degree later taught me that understanding the universe involves rather more maths than looking up at it. Interstellar is still my favourite film, but computers gradually became the other universe I wanted to explore.

After nearly four years at a certain software company in China, I'm back in student mode, studying for an MSc in Computer Science at the University of Liverpool, United Kingdom. My working days involved building and improving software on Linux with colleagues, chasing bugs, and asking why something was slower than it ought to be. GDB and perf were frequent companions. The bugs were less welcome. These days, I'm also learning Python, getting used to life with fewer braces and more meaningful spaces.

I'm curious about C/C++ systems, operating systems, runtimes and memory management, compiler optimisation, and parallel computing and CPU/GPU performance. Networked systems and practical algorithms also tend to send me down a rabbit hole. I like questions I can explore by building something, measuring it, and finding out which assumption was wrong. Alongside my MSc, I'm looking for Computer Science PhD opportunities starting in 2027 in these areas. More systems to understand, more experiments to run, and presumably a few more bugs to negotiate with.

Over the past few years, photography has become another way of paying attention: landscapes, architecture, and people going about their lives. Some moments deserve a photograph before they become a vague “you had to be there.” I also enjoy PC games and investigating gadgets, smart devices and computer hardware. I used to happily disappear into research on headphones and speakers, but I've since retired from chasing the next audio upgrade — my ears and wallet have agreed to a truce.

Away from screens, I still have a soft spot for inline skating and table tennis, favourites from my earlier years, with ice skating and skiing also on the list. Apparently, sitting still was never going to cover all my hobbies. This corner collects the things I learn, make, notice, and haven't quite figured out yet. Expect a little code, a few photographs, and the occasional detour.

02 / Working chapters

C++ Development Engineer

2021 – 2025 · A certain software company · China · C/C++ on Linux

Over nearly four years, I worked on four C/C++ projects on Linux, building and extending modules for data processing, routing, scheduling and system integration. I also tested, deployed and maintained the software, including its build settings, dependencies and operational shell scripts.

I worked with developers and test engineers to trace runtime issues with GDB, perf and Wireshark, and helped new colleagues find their way around the code with diagrams and walkthroughs.

DATA DISTRIBUTION

Getting data to the right place

I took on a Linux data router’s core rewrite, from validation to scheduling, then got the bytes moving with perf, CPU affinity and jemalloc.

SYSTEMS INTEGRATION

Making different systems agree

I integrated Linux C/C++ tools, adapted interfaces and formats, and refereed the occasional argument about byte order.

EARLIER TRAINING PROJECTS

Learning by wiring things together

I helped build resumable file transfer and text search with word suggestions, thread pools and Reactor patterns (the sockets kept things lively).
